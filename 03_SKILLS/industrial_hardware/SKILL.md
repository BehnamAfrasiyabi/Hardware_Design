# Industrial Electronics Hardware Engineering
Status: Draft

## Intake
Identify equipment category, installation class, supply (AC/DC), voltage/current/energy, motor/inductive loads, grounding and protective earth, cable lengths, enclosure/IP rating, pollution degree/overvoltage category/altitude when relevant, ambient temperature, vibration, service life, target market, and safety functions.

## Standards selection
Assess product-specific standards first, then relevant IEC 61000 EMC, IEC 60068 environmental, IEC 60204-1 machinery electrical equipment, IEC 61508 functional safety, IEC 61131-2 programmable controller equipment, and applicable regional requirements. These are candidates only; exact scope and edition must be verified.

## Workflow
1. Define system boundary, modes, interfaces, and failure behavior.
2. Analyze mains/DC input, inrush, brownout, surge, EFT, ESD, reverse polarity, overload, and short-circuit cases as applicable.
3. Define isolation, protective earth, creepage/clearance, insulation system, and thermal requirements from applicable standards and actual conditions.
4. Review EMC filter strategy, cable interfaces, shielding, grounding, common-mode paths, and immunity recovery.
5. Review relay, motor, solenoid, and inductive load switching, suppression, and contact life.
6. Verify component derating, environmental rating, lifecycle, and maintainability.
7. Define production and field diagnostics, test points, calibration, and service strategy.
8. Create a verification matrix tied to requirements and standards clauses.

## Outputs
Industrial environment profile, standards applicability record, power/protection architecture, isolation review, EMC risk report, verification matrix, and manufacturing/service notes.

## Stop conditions
No compliance claim without exact applicable standard editions, installation assumptions, test setup, and evidence.
